# Sets games: rules for the scoring screen

Research date: 13 November 2026. Use the competition's selected format, not an assumed universal club format. A completed rally normally awards a point; a let or ordered replay does not. Record decisions and corrections without inventing a point. The rules below describe ordinary play, not every disciplinary or medical exception.

## Indoor volleyball

Source for this section: [FIVB Official Volleyball Rules 2025-2028](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/FIVB-Volleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v05.pdf), rules cited below.

- **Set and match:** Rules 6.1-6.3: rally winner scores. Sets 1-4 are to 25, at least 2 clear, with no cap. First to 3 sets wins; at 2-2 the fifth set is to 15, at least 2 clear, no cap. Best of 3 is a competition or local format, not the standard match in rule 6.3.
- **Serve and rotation:** Rules 7.6.2 and 12.1-12.2: the serving team winning a rally keeps service; if the receiving team wins, it gains the point and service and rotates clockwise one position before serving. The first service in set 1 and the deciding set follows a toss; in other sets the team that did not serve first in the previous set serves first. A team has 6 players; beach doubles rules do not apply to indoor rotation. A known team server alone does not identify the individual server.
- **Ends:** Rules 18.2.1-18.2.2: change courts after each set, except as provided for the deciding set; in the deciding set change when a team reaches 8, without delay. If missed, change as soon as noticed and keep the score. Competition-specific adaptations can differ.
- **Breaks:** Rules 15.1, 15.4.1 and 18.1: each team has at most 2 requested timeouts per set, normally 30 seconds each; intervals between sets are 3 minutes, with an extension between sets 2 and 3 possible under the stated authority. Rule 15.4 permits approved competition adjustments to timeout length. The July note's automatic 60-second technical timeouts at 8 and 16 are **not verified** in this edition's base rules. Do not make them an automatic FIVB default; check the event regulations.
- **No point on replay:** Rule 6.1.2.2: simultaneous faults by opponents are a double fault and the rally is replayed. Rule 17.2.2: an external interference results in a replay. Rule 12.3.4: service before the referee's whistle is cancelled and repeated. A normal service fault or ball out awards the opponent a point, not a replay (rules 6.1.2-6.1.3).
- **Club options:** 21 or 15 points, match length, deciding target, margin and cap must be explicit event settings. These are local variants, not the above FIVB indoor default; their prevalence and a universal default cap are **not verified**.
- **Beach, one line:** [FIVB Official Beach Volleyball Rules 2025-2028](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FIVB-BeachVolleyball_Rules2025_2028-EN-v01.pdf), rules 6.2-6.3, 15.1 and 18.2: best of 3, 21/21/15, win by 2, change ends every 7 total points in sets 1-2 and every 5 in set 3, with 1 requested 30-second timeout per team per set.

**Engine comparison, source reading only (rung 2):** `src/models/setsRules.js:3-7, 71-85` describes the indoor target, margin and decider; `:7, 88-98` uses manual first-server policy even for sets 2-4, where the rule determines it. `:101-103` tracks team service, not the individual rotation. `src/designs/design1-mono/scoring/MonoSetsLiveScore.jsx:231-234, 281-282` automatically changes displayed sides at the threshold and between sets, without evidence that the players actually changed ends. It permits manual correction at `:583-586`. The inspected rules model contains no timeout allowance or interval fields (`setsRules.js:47-67`); this is not proof that no other screen can record them. Legacy profiles differ (`:38-46, 54-66`).

## Badminton

Primary reference for 2026: [BWF Statutes Section 4.1, Laws of Badminton, version 5.0, effective 26 April 2025](https://extranet.bwf.sport/docs/document-system/81/1466/1470/Section%204.1%20-%20Laws%20of%20Badminton%20-%2026%20April%202025%20V5.0%20(2)%20.pdf); the [BWF-hosted laws text](https://system.bwfbadminton.com/documents/folder_1_81/Statutes/CHAPTER-4---RULES-OF-THE-GAME/SECTION%204.1-%20Laws%20of%20Badminton.pdf) also shows the numbered laws. Check edition and event regulations at use time.

- **2026 standard:** Law 7: best of 3 games, each to 21 with 2 clear after 20-20; at 29-29, first to 30 wins 30-29. Each completed rally gives its winner a point. Law 7.6: game winner serves first in the next game.
- **Serve:** Laws 10.1, 10.3, 11.1, 11.3-11.6: rally winner serves next. Server's side score even, including 0, means right service court; odd means left. In doubles, on winning a rally while serving, the same server serves again from the other court; on a receiving-side win, service passes to that side. Track the initial server, initial receiver and partners: service passes initial server, initial receiver's partner, initial server's partner, initial receiver, then repeats. Players change service courts only after winning a point on their own serve. Either member of the winning pair can serve first next game, and either opponent can receive. Law 12: correct a service-court or order error when discovered; score stands.
- **Ends:** Law 8: change after game 1, after game 2 if a third is needed, and in game 3 when a side reaches 11 under the 21-point system. If missed, change as soon as discovered when the shuttle is not in play; score stands.
- **Intervals:** Law 16.2: up to 60 seconds in each 21-point game when the leading score reaches 11; up to 120 seconds between games. The ordinary laws do not provide a discretionary team timeout as in volleyball; suspension and exceptional circumstances are separate (law 16.3).
- **No point on a let:** Law 14: repeat the rally, with the last server serving again; examples include receiver not ready, simultaneous service faults, shuttle caught on or in net after passing it on return, shuttle disintegrating, unsighted line judge and umpire unable to decide, or unforeseen accident. Ordinary service and playing faults award a point to the opponent (laws 7.3, 13); do not treat every fault as a let. A serve touching the net is not by itself a let.
- **15-point and uncapped variants:** [BWF Alternative Laws, Section 4.1.4](https://system.bwfbadminton.com/documents/folder_1_81/Statutes/CHAPTER-4---RULES-OF-THE-GAME/Section%204.1.4%20-%20Alternative%20Laws%20of%20Badminton.pdf), alternative 2, laws 7, 8.1.3 and 16.2.1, permits by prior arrangement best of 3 to 15, 2 clear from 14-14, capped at 21, interval and deciding-game end change at 8. An uncapped 15- or 21-point game is a custom club choice, not this BWF alternative. Its prevalence is **not verified**.

### Change scheduled for 2027

**Primary-source verification limit:** BWF's own [25 April 2026 approval announcement](https://bwfbadminton.com/news-single/2026/04/25/bwf-members-approve-3x15-scoring-system/) and [26 April explanation](https://bwfbadminton.com/news-single/2026/04/26/key-changes-under-the-3x15-scoring-system/) returned HTTP 402 when opened. The [Japan Badminton Association notice](https://www.badminton.or.jp/corporate/news/detail/2156) links the first and reports approval and 4 January 2027. The BWF-authored [2027 Alternative Laws](https://ghanabadminton.org/wp-content/uploads/Section-4.1.4-Alternative-Laws-of-Badminton-04-January-2027-V2.0.pdf), hosted by Ghana's association, are labelled effective that date and list 3 x 21 as an alternative. Neither substitutes for directly reading the BWF announcement or 2027 main law. Therefore the effective date and full new default are **not verified directly from BWF's accessible site**; the reported change is strongly corroborated, not independently confirmed to the requested standard.

The reported new standard is best of 3 to 15, 2 clear from 14-14, with 21-20 the cap; up to 60 seconds at 8, change ends at 8 in game 3, and up to 120 seconds between games. These numbers match BWF's existing 15-point alternative and the [Badminton Denmark announcement](https://badminton.dk/2026/05/28/3x15-pointsystem-indfoeres-efter-sommerferien/), but their application as the 2027 main law is **not verified** from the accessible BWF main law. Note that the 2027 alternative document's 21-point subsection says interval at 8, so do not silently assume its interval is 11 without checking the final text and event rules.

**Recommended app default:** use the 2025 BWF 3 x 21 profile in 2026, offer 3 x 15 by selection, and prepare a versioned 3 x 15 profile for 4 January 2027, subject to direct verification of BWF's operative 2027 laws before automatic activation. Let the scorer choose the competition format; freeze it for an existing match. Some associations adopt earlier, so calendar date alone does not establish a local event's rules.

The profile is static, with no scheduled 2027 profile at `:2-21`; custom target alone retains the profile cap and deciding target unless separately changed (`:34-43, 54`). The inspected model represents team server, not individual doubles positions or service courts (`:47-64, 101-115`). `src/designs/design1-mono/scoring/MonoSetsLiveScore.jsx:231-234, 281-282` swaps displayed sides automatically; the model has no interval state in `setsRules.js:47-67`. These are observations of these paths, not a full runtime audit.

## Table tennis

Source: [ITTF Statutes 2026, Laws of Table Tennis and Regulations for International Competitions](https://db.ittf.com/sites/default/files/public/2026-02/2026_Statutes_v1_consolidated_tc.pdf), laws 2 and regulations 3; check the [ITTF statutes page](https://www.ittf.com/statutes/) for subsequent changes.

- **Game and match:** Laws 2.11-2.12: first to 11 wins, except at 10-10 first 2-point lead wins, no cap. Match is best of any odd number of games; 5 and 7 are possible competition choices, not a single mandatory number in the law.
- **Service:** Laws 2.13.1-2.13.6: after each 2 points the receiving player or pair becomes server. At 10-10, or under expedite, service alternates every point. First server and receiver are chosen initially by lot; the player or pair serving first in one game receives first in the next. In doubles the first serving pair chooses its server and the other pair chooses receiver; each subsequent server is the preceding receiver, and the partner of the preceding server becomes receiver. In later games the first receiver is the player who served to that game's first server in the preceding game. In the last possible game the receiving pair changes its receiving order when either pair reaches 5. Doubles service must go from server's right half court to receiver's right half (law 2.6.3).
- **Expedite:** Law 2.15: after 10 minutes of play in a game, unless at least 18 points have already been scored; it may also be introduced at any time at both players' or pairs' request. If the ball is in play, interrupt and repeat with that rally's server; if not, the previous receiver serves. Thereafter service alternates every point; receiver scores if the receiving player or pair makes 13 correct returns. It remains in force to match end and does not change the doubles service order. A timer and return counter are needed if offering this mode.
- **Ends and mistakes:** Laws 2.13.7 and 2.14: players change ends after each game and in the last possible game when either reaches 5. On discovering a missed end change, interrupt and change as soon as discovered; points already scored stand. Correct wrong service or receiving order using the order established at the beginning of the match and the current score; all points before discovery stand.
- **Intervals:** Regulations 3.4.4.1-3.4.4.2: up to 1 minute between games; brief towelling after every 6 points and at the final-game end change. Each player or pair may claim 1 timeout of up to 1 minute per individual match, between rallies. No automatic technical timeout is specified in those provisions.
- **Lets and points:** Laws 2.9-2.10: a let is unscored and replayed, including a correct service touching the net, receiver not ready provided no attempt to return is made, a disturbance outside the player's control, or play interrupted by the umpire. Ordinary failure to make a correct service or return awards a point to the opponent. A net touch in a rally alone is not automatically a let.
- **Club option:** 21-point games are a local or historical format, not current ITTF law. Agree margin, serve rotation and deciding end change explicitly; do not infer all historical rules from target 21. The prevalence of this variant is **not verified**.

**Engine comparison, source reading only (rung 2):** `src/models/setsRules.js:15-19, 57, 101-115` represents 11, uncapped win by 2, 2-point service blocks and single-point deuce, plus team-level first-server alternation. The model does not represent player-level doubles server/receiver order or an expedite switch (`:47-67, 101-115`); no interval or timeout field appears in that model. `src/designs/design1-mono/scoring/MonoSetsLiveScore.jsx:231-234, 281-282` automatically switches displayed ends, while law 2.14 handles an actual missed switch. Do not assume a displayed swap confirms physical compliance.
