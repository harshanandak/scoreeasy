Taps per ball. A normal ball or a boundary is 1 tap — the number commits and rotates the strike for you. A plain wide or no-ball is 1 tap. A wide with runs, or a bye/leg-bye, is 2 taps: arm the modifier, then tap the number. A wicket is 2 taps. Undo sits next to the confirm button on every screen, and a 150 ms debounce catches double-taps.
Extras never hide behind a menu here, which is what makes Power mode fast — and also why it isn't the default. If a ball ever looks wrong on the over strip, tap undo rather than scoring around it.

The escalation ladder. A dot is a silent tick. A single flips the numeral. A four washes the hero green. A six adds an edge burst. A wicket turns the strip red and snaps the momentum line to the bowling side. Fifty and a hundred earn the gold card — one per screen, ever. Every step lands in under half a second and is switched off entirely when reduced motion is on.
None of it blocks the keypad. A Power-mode scorer can keep tapping straight through any celebration; the card waits in the timeline rather than stopping the ledger.
