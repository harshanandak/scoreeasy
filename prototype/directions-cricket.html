ScoreEasy · cricket scorer · design directions

The hardest screen, three ways

Cricket is the densest scorer we have — runs, wickets, overs, two batters, a bowler, the current over, the chase maths and the extras pad all live on one thumb-reachable screen. If a design language survives cricket, it survives every other sport. Same ball, same moment, three languages.

DIRECTION A
Floodlight
A stadium jumbotron in your hand. Near-black, one electric amber, monumental condensed numerals (Teko). Reads across a noisy ground from arm's length — built for the person actually scoring.
Cricket test: the total owns the screen; batters, bowler and the over track read as a quiet instrument panel beneath it.

DIRECTION B
Ledger
The cricket scorebook every Indian school already owns — cream paper, the red margin rule of a ruled register, Playfair ink serifs. Warm, credible, unmistakably ours. A record, not an app screen.
Cricket test: this is the one language cricket invented — the batting card and the over register are literally the book's own rows and boxes.

DIRECTION C
Court
Loud and young. Saturated colour blocks, thick black frames, hard offset shadows, chunky Archivo. Feels like a sticker on a college notice board — energetic, but disciplined, not chaotic.
Cricket test: the strike batter is highlighted in yellow, the bowler is a black bar, and every run button is a physical key you can hit without looking.
